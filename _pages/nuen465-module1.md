A material property is part of a larger engineering story. Processing changes structure; structure influences properties; those properties help determine performance in a particular environment. Module 1 uses this sequence to connect nuclear materials selection with computational design.

## 1. Start with the material's function

Fuel, cladding, structural materials, moderators, and coolants serve different roles within a coupled reactor system. Material selection must account for temperature, stress, chemistry, irradiation, and fabrication history together. The module compares oxide, nonoxide ceramic, metallic, coated-particle, and liquid fuel forms as engineering tradeoffs.

## 2. Choose a scale that resolves the mechanism

Electronic, atomistic, microstructural, and component models answer different questions. A useful multiscale workflow explains which information passes between those levels and why it is sufficient for the intended decision. The module introduces both hierarchical transfer of material descriptions and concurrent exchange between coupled models.

## 3. Connect computation with evidence

Integrated computational materials engineering (ICME) connects processing, material structure, properties, and performance through models, experiments, and data. For nuclear applications, the material's evolution during service is part of this picture. Qualification requires traceable evidence about the material and the conditions under which it must function.

Verification, validation, and uncertainty are distinct parts of that evidence. Numerical consistency does not by itself establish agreement with physical behavior, and agreement under one set of conditions does not establish applicability everywhere.

## 4. Make the study reproducible

The computational workflow links problem definition, governing equations, boundary conditions, numerical solution, visualization, and interpretation. MOOSE, scientific notebooks, field visualization, and version control provide complementary capabilities. The emphasis is on preserving the reasoning and evidence behind a result so that another person can inspect it.

## What to take away

After working through the module, you should be able to explain a nuclear material's intended function, identify mechanisms that could compromise it in service, choose useful modeling scales, and outline an experimental–computational evidence plan for a design or qualification question.
