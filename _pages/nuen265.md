NUEN265 introduces materials science for nuclear-energy applications, covering metals, ceramics and polymers. It connects atomic-scale structure to material properties, performance and failure in demanding environments.

The course progresses from atomic structure and bonding through crystal structures, defects and solid-state diffusion, then examines thermal and mechanical behavior, elasticity, plasticity, strengthening, grain growth, fracture, fatigue and creep. Phase diagrams, including the Fe–C system, connect composition to material behavior. Radiation damage and irradiation effects are considered throughout. Students apply simple mechanical, thermal, kinetic and thermodynamic models, with introductory MOOSE simulations, to analyze materials performance and support materials selection.

The resources below support lectures and independent study and will expand as additional course topics are developed.

## Crystal structures · Part I: Elemental solids

Use rotatable structures, illustrations, and calculators to connect a crystal's geometry to its physical properties.

- **Lattices and symmetry:** lattice points, atomic bases, the Bravais lattices, and why some proposed lattices are not distinct types.
- **Stacking and unit cells:** FCC versus HCP, shared atoms, coordination, and packing.
- **Volume and density:** contact directions, cell dimensions, and worked examples for elemental metals.
- **Nuclear metals:** allotropy and the structures of uranium, plutonium, and thorium.

## Crystal structures · Part II: Compounds and alloys

Build on elemental structures to understand how different species occupy crystal sites. Start with a complete crystal, then use optional step-by-step construction, rotatable views and calculations to examine its geometry.

- **Ion sizes and interstitial sites:** radius ratios, coordination, sublattices, and tetrahedral/octahedral sites in FCC, BCC and HCP.
- **Compound structures:** rock salt, CsCl, zinc blende, fluorite, corundum and perovskite, with nuclear examples including UC, UN, UO₂, ThO₂ and PuO₂.
- **Alloys and ordering:** distinguish positional order from chemical order or disorder in solid solutions and ordered compounds.
- **Counting and density:** formula units, site ownership, worked calculations and questions with hints and explanations.
