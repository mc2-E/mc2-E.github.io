NUEN465 applies materials-science principles to nuclear-energy systems. The course connects crystal structures, defects, metallurgy, and materials thermochemistry to nuclear fuel performance, structural-material changes, and waste materials.

## Course overview

The Fall 2026 syllabus combines materials fundamentals with computational practice. Students study thermodynamics and kinetics, the effects of defects and irradiation, and the relationships among composition, microstructure, and properties. Multiscale and multiphysics models connect those mechanisms to behavior under high temperature, stress, and irradiation.

The course introduces the finite-element method and MOOSE, then develops thermal and mechanical modeling, effective properties of heterogeneous materials, and phase-field descriptions of microstructure evolution. Later syllabus topics include stress–microstructure coupling, defect balance laws, irradiation-induced segregation, and swelling. These topics describe the course as a whole; the learning collection below begins with Module 1.

### Learning goals

- Explain the materials challenges of fission and fusion systems.
- Relate defects, composition, and microstructure to material properties and performance.
- Implement material models with MOOSE and study nuclear-fuel behavior at microstructure and pellet scales.
- Check models against analytical relationships or experimental observations and communicate a technical study clearly.

This overview summarizes the Fall 2026 syllabus. Enrolled students should use Canvas for the current course requirements and assigned materials.

## Module 1 · Nuclear Materials and Computational Design

Start with the materials argument behind a reactor design: what a material must do, how processing and service conditions change it, and what evidence is needed to trust a prediction.

- **Materials reasoning:** processing → structure → properties → performance.
- **Nuclear applications:** fuel, cladding, structures, moderators, and coolants as a coupled materials system.
- **Modeling across scales:** connect atomistic mechanisms, microstructure, and component response.
- **Credible decisions:** qualification, integrated computational materials engineering (ICME), verification, validation, uncertainty, and reproducibility.
