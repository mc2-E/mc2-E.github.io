  <h2 id="thermal-conductivity-models">From experiments to fast thermal-conductivity models</h2>
  <p><strong>Fergany Badry, Merve Gencturk, and Karim Ahmed</strong> · <em>Journal of Nuclear Engineering</em> 7(1), 8 · Published January 22, 2026</p>
  <p><em>An Integrated Approach for Generating Reduced Order Models of the Effective Thermal Conductivity of Nuclear Fuels</em></p>
  <p>How can a limited set of measurements support fast predictions of heat transport? This study connects experiments, mesoscale finite-element models, and machine-learning regression for UO₂–BeO and UO₂–Mo composites and U-10Zr metallic fuel.</p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Check the physics against measurements.</strong> Mesoscale models account for microstructure and interfacial thermal resistance and are compared with experimental conductivity data.</li>
    <li><strong>Use each data source deliberately.</strong> Experimental data train the regressors; additional mesoscale calculations supply testing data at different input combinations.</li>
    <li><strong>Build a compact property model.</strong> Multi-polynomial regression provides an analytical expression suitable for use in engineering-scale calculations.</li>
  </ol>
  <p>On the reported simulation-based tests, the selected polynomial model had maximum deviations of 10% for UO₂–BeO, 7% for UO₂–Mo, and 9% for U-10Zr. These comparisons concern effective thermal conductivity in the studied systems. Predictions at extended temperature and composition ranges are not independent experimental validation; irradiation-driven evolution and transient coupling remain future work.</p>
