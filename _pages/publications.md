<section aria-labelledby="fe-cr-segregation">
  <h2 id="fe-cr-segregation">Why chromium enriches or depletes at grain boundaries</h2>
  <p><strong>Russell Oplinger, Mukesh Bachhav, Karim Ahmed, and Sourabh Bhagwan Kadambi</strong> · <em>Journal of Applied Physics</em> 139(15), 155903 (2026)</p>
  <p><em>Radiation-induced segregation in dilute Fe–Cr: A rate-theory framework for the Cr enrichment–depletion transition at the grain boundary</em></p>
  <p>We collaborated on a rate-theory model of Fe–0.1 at.% Cr. Under symmetric defect production and absorption, the model recovers chromium enrichment at lower temperatures and depletion at higher temperatures. Preferential effective vacancy production or preferential interstitial absorption shifts this transition toward lower temperatures, showing why transport coefficients alone are insufficient when defect fluxes are asymmetric.</p>

<section aria-labelledby="stress-corrosion-316h">
  <h2 id="stress-corrosion-316h">Mapping stress-dependent corrosion in molten salt</h2>
  <p><strong>Kyle S. Williams, Rijul R. Chauhan, Kenneth Cooper, Nathaniel Thomas, Karim Ahmed, and Lin Shao</strong> · <em>Materials Today Communications</em> 49, 114225 (2025)</p>
  <p><em>Combining four-point bending and corrosion to map stress-dependent corrosion susceptibility of 316H stainless steel in FLiNaK</em></p>
  <p>We collaborated on an experiment linking local stress to corrosion in 316H stainless steel. Four-point bending, guided by finite-element analysis, provided tensile and compressive regions during a 100-hour exposure to FLiNaK at 700 °C. Tensile regions showed deeper attack and greater grain-boundary chromium depletion, demonstrating the value of resolving stress when comparing corrosion susceptibility.</p>

  <p><strong>Anthony Albert Harrup, Mathias Boulanger, Sarah Cole, Mehmet Topsakal, Laura K. Sudderth, Karim Ahmed, Tiankai Yao, and Ericmoore Jossou</strong> · <em>Journal of Nuclear Materials</em> 634, 157082 (2026)</p>
  <p><em>Effects of processing technique on microstructural evolution of U–Zr alloy: A combined XRD and SEM analysis</em></p>
  <p>With our collaborators, we found that annealing reduced microstrain in extruded material, while quenched material retained microstrain similar to the as-extruded condition. We connect these observations to the influence of fabrication history on U–10Zr microstructure.</p>

<section aria-labelledby="thermal-conductivity-models">
  <h2 id="thermal-conductivity-models">From experiments to fast thermal-conductivity models</h2>
  <p><strong>Fergany Badry, Merve Gencturk, and Karim Ahmed</strong> · <em>Journal of Nuclear Engineering</em> 7(1), 8 · Published January 22, 2026</p>
  <p><em>An Integrated Approach for Generating Reduced Order Models of the Effective Thermal Conductivity of Nuclear Fuels</em></p>
  <p>We connect experimental conductivity data, mesoscale finite-element models and machine-learning regression to develop fast property predictions for UO₂–BeO and UO₂–Mo composites and U-10Zr metallic fuel.</p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Check the physics against measurements.</strong> We account for microstructure and interfacial thermal resistance in mesoscale models and compare their predictions with experimental conductivity data.</li>
    <li><strong>Use each data source deliberately.</strong> We train regressors on experimental data and use additional mesoscale calculations to test them at different input combinations.</li>
    <li><strong>Build a compact property model.</strong> We use multi-polynomial regression to obtain an analytical expression suitable for engineering-scale calculations.</li>
  </ol>
  <p>On the reported simulation-based tests, our selected polynomial model had maximum deviations of 10% for UO₂–BeO, 7% for UO₂–Mo, and 9% for U-10Zr. These comparisons concern effective thermal conductivity in the studied systems. Predictions at extended temperature and composition ranges are not independent experimental validation; irradiation-driven evolution and transient coupling remain future work.</p>

</section>
