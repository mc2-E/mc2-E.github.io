Which electrical quantity is controlled?

  • Prescribed actual Cr overpotential: E follows E_Cr,eq + η_Cr,set. Fe uses this same E. Vacancy shifts of E_Cr,eq do not directly suppress the prescribed Cr voltage factor.
  • Prescribed common E: E is fixed relative to the original Cr reference. Both actual metal overpotentials evolve.
  • Maintained-chemistry mixed potential: E is solved from current balance. All bath activities remain fixed; depositing Fe can be supplied by the bath. This is an open-system bath model.
  • Finite-bath mixed potential: the same current balance holds, but separate Fe/Cr inventories and shared HF/H₂ evolve with the signed currents. All reaction-law presets and editable kinetic/reference parameters remain available.

Let Qᵢ be signed molar transfer from alloy to bath, not electrical charge. Cb denotes the shared reaction capacity. The initial HF and H₂ inventories are 2Cb and Cb. Define uᵢ = Qᵢ/Cb and U = u_Cr + u_Fe.

The implicit boundary solve updates these inventories from each trial current in the same time step. It uses the selected generalized BV activity powers, not a separate source-law approximation. Initial activity ratios, reference gaps and inventories are distinct inputs; avoid counting the same chemical shift twice.

What “finite-bath equilibrium” means

Every active partial current vanishes, so each product inventory stops changing and the alloy returns to its uniform fixed-reservoir composition. Zero net current alone is insufficient: Cr dissolution balanced by Fe deposition would still change both inventories. The equilibrium is solved separately; two years is not assumed long enough to reach it.

ECr,eq=EFe,eq=EH,eq

Omit the Fe equality when its reaction is disabled. With positive exchange-current prefactors, changing the kinetic law changes the approach, but not the equilibrium condition. Equilibrium accumulated transfer is constrained by inventories and the fixed far-alloy reservoir; it can exceed the Cr initially within the modeled alloy length. It is not surface recession.

Stationary profiles are affine and their boundary root is solved independently. Transients use the exact finite-slab diffusion response with implicit, piecewise-constant flux integration and positive-composition boundary roots. A converged stationary root does not establish uniqueness or dynamical stability. The stationary state is not assumed to have been reached at two years. Use the fine time grid to check a sensitive or nearly cancelling result.

The three kinetic presets are sensitivity alternatives. A prefactor switch retains the displayed j_ref and α values, so “linear metal activity” is not automatically the complete historical Cr-only model. Arbitrary powers, large voltages and reference gaps may produce no resolvable positive state. A numerical failure is reported without claiming physical nonexistence.

Evidence and physical scope

The approved starting scales adapt Wu, Guo & Zhang (2017) pure-metal FLiNaK kinetics using the retained alloy and salt reference mapping. The Fe–Cr gap uses Wang, Sridharan & Couet (2021) thermodynamics with a consistent alloy-reference correction. The generalized activity prefactor follows the framework discussed by Bazant (2013).

Reference dissolved MF₂ activities are 10⁻⁶; the experimental-to-alloy mapping assumes estimated fluoride activity coefficients and a unit barrier-transfer factor. These are conditional alloy coefficients, not measured 316 corrosion rates. The source kinetic fits span approximately ±150 mV about each metal equilibrium; wider overpotentials extrapolate those fits. Bulk coefficients frozen far from the surface composition are an additional approximation.

The finite-bath option adds the explicitly selected Fe and Cr inventories. The equal default product inventories are an approved sensitivity case, not measured salt chemistry; the 450 mV hydrogen reference remains the retained model preset. Existing MOOSE results remain independent comparison evidence and are not re-simulated by this calculator.
