Two different long-time limits

Finite closed bath: a stationary bath requires zero net reaction rate. Alloy profiles return to the fixed far-boundary composition and cumulative removal approaches a finite endpoint. HF need not be fully exhausted.

Maintained chemistry: a stationary alloy can sustain a nonzero Cr flux between the far-alloy and salt reservoirs. Its Ni, Cr and vacancy profiles are linear for constant D. Cumulative removal keeps increasing and has no finite stationary endpoint.

These limits are calculated independently. A two-year trajectory need not have reached either limit. Cumulative equivalent removal can exceed L when the far-alloy reservoir replenishes Cr; this does not imply wall motion.

What this calculator solves

This is a semi-analytical benchmark. It evaluates the bulk diffusion response analytically and solves the nonlinear surface reaction history numerically. The mobility B and thermodynamic Hessian H are evaluated at the chosen initial reference and held constant. Surface activities retain the full nonlinear model. This hybrid isolates the effect of freezing bulk transport; it is not the fully nonlinear MOOSE model.

Bulk transport and boundaries

q = (xNi, xCr, xv)ᵀ; xFe = 1 − xNi − xCr − xv
∂q/∂t = D ∂²q/∂x²; D = BH
D ∂q/∂x(0,t) = (0,1,−1)ᵀ r(t)
q(x,0) = qb; q(L,t) = qb

Both models retain the full H. “Thermodynamic only” (PT) uses diagonal B; “thermodynamic + kinetic” (PTK) uses full B. D is generally not diagonal in either case. Positive r removes Cr and injects vacancies. Ni has zero surface flux but can redistribute through coupling.

The initial and far vacancy fraction is Sv × 2.91878 × 10⁻⁸. All metal fractions scale proportionally to preserve Fe:Ni:Cr ratios among occupied sites. Thermal activity and mobility normalizations stay fixed.

The shared mixed-potential interface

A = aCr/aCr,thermal and V = av/av,thermal are evaluated at the evolving alloy surface. Salt activity ratios H (HF), P (H₂) and S (dissolved Cr(II)) enter both interface laws. S is distinct from alloy vacancy supersaturation Sv.

ECr = (VT/2) ln(VS/A)
EH = ΔE⁰ + VT(ln H − ½ ln P)
jCr = 2j⁰CrA sinh[(E − ECr)/VT]
jH = 2j⁰H√(H√P) sinh[(E − EH)/(2VT)]
jCr + jH = 0;   equivalent removal = ∫ r dt

VT = RT/F = 83.8596 mV; ΔE⁰ = 0.45 V; j⁰Cr = j⁰H = 5 A/m². These are inherited model inputs, not experimental measurements established by this calculator. The potential and both reaction overpotentials are solved, not prescribed.

Finite closed bath

u = Q/Qcapacity,   u(0) = 0
H = 1 − u;   P = 1 + u;   S = 1 + 10u
dQ/dt = jCr/(2F)

Q is molar reaction extent per electrode area, not electrical charge. Qcapacity = 1.885073792 mol/m² is the same for every alloy length. Alloy size changes the diffusion problem, not the prescribed amount of salt per area. At final equilibrium, r = 0 and q = qb. With K = exp(2ΔE⁰/VT)Ab/Vb, the exact endpoint is ueq = 2(K−1)/[2K+11+√(88K+81)].

Maintained chemistry / infinite bath

H = P = S = 1 at all times

These ratios denote the original nonzero reference activities. There is no capacity or inventory ODE. An external reservoir supplies reactants and removes products, approximating negligible local chemistry change or effective chemical renewal. This does not calculate salt flow or boundary-layer transport.

Maintaining salt chemistry does not remove alloy feedback. Vacancy accumulation and Cr depletion still raise ECr and can strongly suppress corrosion in both cases.

Analytical response, numerical reaction history

q(x,t) = qb − ∫₀ᵗ G(x,t−τ) r(τ) dτ
G̃i(0,p) = tanh[L√(p/λi)] / √(λip)

λi are the positive eigenvalues of D; modal weights project the Cr-removal/vacancy-injection flux onto each diffusion mode. Image and eigenfunction series evaluate the integrated kernel. The reaction rate is linear within each small time interval, with the new rate found implicitly from both Butler–Volmer laws and, for the finite bath, its inventory balance. This time discretization has finite error; it is checked by temporal refinement in the accompanying QA files.

The long-time limits are calculated separately. Transient histories are never replaced by that endpoint. There is no spatial mesh, additional phase, capacitance, electrostatic field, finite renewal time, or void-formation law.

Meaning of the reported removal

r = 10⁶jCr/(2FCsite) in µm/s, with Csite = 10⁵ mol/m³. The cumulative integral is equivalent Cr removal: Cr moles removed per area divided by total lattice site density. It does not directly predict wall recession or Cr-depletion depth. The rate in µm/year uses 365 days/year; a negative rate means net Cr deposition.
