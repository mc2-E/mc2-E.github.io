What model is being calculated?

This is the established linear analytical prescribed-overpotential benchmark: r = h cs, where h = j₀ sinh(η/VT)/(F Csite cb). It holds the actual Cr overpotential fixed and uses j₀ = 5 A/m² as a diagnostic reaction scale. It does not use the alternative square-root activity prefactor from the separate BV sensitivity study.

The full thermodynamic Hessian H is retained. PT uses diagonal kinetic mobility B; PTK includes its vacancy-exchange cross terms. For each trial, D = BH is evaluated at that trial’s uniform initial/far composition and then held constant in space.

a=(BH)−1(0,1,−1)T

Deff = 1/aCr; kD = Deff/L; kI = h.
qs = qb − L a r, for q = (Ni, Cr, vacancy).

Cr tracer diffusivity is not Deff. Scaling D*Cr rebuilds all linked Cr mobility terms, including vacancy transport. Fe/Ni tracer values remain fixed. Changing Sᵥ reevaluates both H and B while preserving occupied-site metal ratios and the thermal vacancy normalization.

Units and physical scope

Conductances k have units m/s, resistances R have units s/m, and r = j/(2 F Csite) is Cr-equivalent removal speed. Multiply m/s by 10⁶ × 31,536,000 to obtain µm/year. This measures removed Cr inventory per area, not predicted wall recession.

The requested ranges are sensitivity windows. Sᵥ = 1,000,000 corresponds to a bulk vacancy fraction of 0.02919 (2.919%). The model has no void formation or vacancy sinks; very high vacancy states, especially near or above 1%, are mathematical extrapolations. Increasing D*Cr by up to one million is likewise not a literature uncertainty range.

Reference material values and reproduction

The benchmark inherits the 316L/FLiNaK parameter set at 700 °C, the thermodynamic Hessian and mobility construction from the target-rate and prescribed analytical explorers. Reference Ni and Cr fractions are 0.1174401 and 0.1885074. Csite = 100,000 mol/m³. Those choices are retained for comparison and are not fitted here.
