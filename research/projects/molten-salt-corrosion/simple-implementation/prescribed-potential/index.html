Surface values and actual reference fractions
Physical thermal calibration stays fixed. vₜₕ, tracer diffusivities and vacancy reference energy remain unchanged. At each S, vᵦ = S vₜₕ, metal site fractions preserve the same occupied-site Fe:Ni:Cr ratios, and both H and B are reevaluated and then frozen. S > 1 is an imposed supersaturated reservoir, not a new thermal equilibrium. A larger S does not by itself make the frozen-coefficient approximation more accurate.

Equations and scope

1 · Thermodynamics and transport

n, c and v are Ni, Cr and vacancy site fractions; Fe = 1 − n − c − v. Both models keep the same full quadratic free energy.

δζ=HδqD=1012BHqt=Dqxx

PT removes kinetic off-diagonal terms from B. Its D is still generally full because H remains full. PTK uses B with negative Ni–v and Cr–v entries. The factor 10¹² converts m² to µm².

2 · Initial and boundary conditions

q(x,0)=q(L,t)=qbDqx(0,t)=srs=(0,1,−1)T

Positive r removes Cr and injects vacancies in equal site-normalized amounts. Ni has zero surface flux. The far boundary fixes all three fractions; it can supply metal and remove vacancies.

Why Ni redistributes. Zero Ni surface flux does not impose a zero Ni concentration gradient. In PT, JNi = −Bnn(Hnn∂n/∂x + Hnc∂c/∂x + Hnv∂v/∂x), with consistent SI units. Ni gradients can therefore balance the Cr and vacancy contributions even when Ni flux vanishes. PTK also includes the kinetic cross terms. During the transient, Ni can flow within the alloy; at steady state its flux is zero everywhere while its profile can remain nonuniform. Ni is computed as an independent site fraction, not by renormalizing the remaining metals. Ni plots use linear vertical axes to show its redistribution clearly.

3 · Prescribed-overpotential surface law

r=hcsh=106j0sinh(FηRT)FCsitecb

η is actual overpotential, held fixed; two-electron Cr dissolution and symmetric transfer coefficient 0.5 are retained. j₀ = 5 A/m² is the inherited diagnostic input; Csite = 10⁵ mol/m³. h has units µm/s. This is a concentration-based analytical boundary law, not the nonlinear activity law or a mixed-potential calculation.

The rate combines surface resistance 1/h and bulk resistance L ac. Their ratio is Da = h L ac. The displayed bulk share is Da/(1 + Da), a resistance measure for this linear steady problem.

How the time-dependent solution is evaluated

The constant matrix D has positive eigenvalues dᵢ. Diagonalizing D = V diag(dᵢ)V⁻¹ reduces the spatial Laplace problem to three diffusion modes. With kᵢ = √(p/dᵢ), the response operator is

G=Vdiag[sinh(ki(L−x))pdicosh(kiL)]V−1

The transformed rate is r̂ = h cᵦ / {p[1 + h eCrᵀ G(p,0)s]}, and δq̂ = −G s r̂. A 24-term Talbot inversion evaluates the exact transform; there is no spatial mesh or PDE time step. The steady term is removed before inversion to improve late-time accuracy. Initial values and zero overpotential are handled directly.

Inspect the selected coefficients and reference state
What the approximation can miss. Small absolute v does not imply a small perturbation about thermal equilibrium. Frozen B and H omit the strong vacancy–transport feedback. A larger reference supersaturation improves neither accuracy nor validity automatically. Cr can also move far from its expansion point. These are analytical benchmarks, not validated alloy-service predictions.
Source records and fixed inputs

Coefficients are evaluated from the project’s full thermodynamic and mobility equations at each selected initial/far-boundary state. The size, supersaturation and voltage presets match the light full-model sweep.
